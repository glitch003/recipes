# Classic French Crêpes

Yield: ~16 crêpes (9–10-inch / 23–25 cm pan)

Thin, tender, buttery crêpes made in a blender. The base is Jennifer Segal's [Once Upon a Chef classic French crêpes](https://www.onceuponachef.com/recipes/crepes.html): blender method, melted butter in the batter, and a rest in the fridge. From Chef JB's [RecipeTin Eats crêpes](https://www.recipetineats.com/crepes/) comes a splash of water, which makes them thinner and lacier, and the trick of wiping the pan with a paper towel so there's only a film of butter. The batter works for sweet or savory crêpes; see Notes.

## Ingredients

- 480 g whole milk (2 cups), room temp or slightly warm
- 60 g water (¼ cup)
- 4 large eggs (~200 g without shells), room temp
- 25 g granulated sugar (2 Tbsp); use 12 g (1 Tbsp) for savory
- 5 g vanilla extract (1 tsp), optional; skip for savory
- 3 g fine salt (½ tsp)
- 190 g all-purpose flour, spooned and leveled (1½ cups)
- 56 g unsalted butter, melted and slightly cooled (4 Tbsp), plus ~10 g more for the pan

## Nutrition (estimated)

- **Per crêpe (1 of 16, plain)**: **~115 kcal**: **Fat 5.6 g**, **Carbs 12 g**, **Protein 3.8 g**, **Fiber 0.3 g**, **Sugar 3.1 g**, **Sodium ~105 mg**
- **Per full batch**: **~1,850 kcal**: **Fat 90 g**, **Carbs 194 g**, **Protein 61 g**, **Fiber 5 g**, **Sugar 50 g**, **Sodium ~1,650 mg**
- **Assumptions**: whole milk, unsalted butter, ~10 g butter used to grease the pan, vanilla included. Fillings not counted.

## Instructions

1. Add **480 g milk**, **60 g water**, **4 eggs**, **25 g sugar**, **5 g vanilla**, and **3 g salt** to a blender. Blend 5–10 seconds.

2. Add **190 g flour** and blend about 30 seconds, until smooth. Scrape down any flour stuck to the sides and pulse again.

3. With the blender running on low, pour in **56 g melted butter** and blend about 10 seconds more.

4. Cover and refrigerate at least 30 minutes, ideally 1–2 hours, or up to 24 hours. Resting lets the flour hydrate and the bubbles settle, so the crêpes come out tender, not rubbery, and are less likely to tear. Before cooking, stir gently. The batter should be about as thick as heavy cream. If it's thicker, whisk in water 1 Tbsp at a time.

5. Heat a 9–10-inch nonstick skillet or crêpe pan over medium heat for a couple of minutes, until a drop of water sizzles right away. Add a small pat of butter (about ½ tsp), let it melt, then wipe it around with a paper towel so only a thin film is left. Pools of butter keep the batter from spreading evenly.

6. Lift the pan off the heat and pour about **60 ml (¼ cup) batter** into the center. Immediately tilt and swirl the pan so the batter coats the bottom in a thin, even layer. Fill any holes with a few drops of batter.

7. Cook about 1 minute, until the top looks dry and set, the edges start to lift and turn lacy, and the underside is golden in spots. Loosen the edge with a thin offset spatula or butter knife, then flip with the spatula or your fingertips. Cook the second side 20–30 seconds; it will be spottier and paler, so it goes on the inside when you fill.

8. Slide onto a plate and stack the crêpes as you go so they stay soft. Re-butter the pan every 2–3 crêpes, or whenever they start to stick. The first crêpe is usually a throwaway that seasons the pan. If they're browning too fast, turn the heat down a notch. If they take more than 90 seconds to set, turn it up.

## Storage

Stack the cooled crêpes with parchment between them, wrap tightly, and refrigerate up to 3 days or freeze up to 1 month. Reheat in a dry skillet over low heat, about 15–30 seconds per side, or wrap the stack in foil and warm at 300°F (150°C) for 10 minutes. Uncooked batter keeps 1–2 days in the fridge; stir and thin it before using.

## Notes

- **Pan**: The bottom of the pan sets the size of the crêpe. With an 8-inch pan, use ~45 ml (3 Tbsp) batter per crêpe and expect ~20 crêpes.
- **No blender**: Whisk the flour, sugar, and salt in a bowl and make a well. Whisk in the eggs to form a thick paste, then add the milk and water gradually, whisking after each addition so you don't get lumps. Whisk in the melted butter last. If it's still lumpy, strain it through a sieve.
- **Savory crêpes**: Cut the sugar to 12 g (1 Tbsp) and leave out the vanilla. Add 1–2 Tbsp chopped chives or herbs after blending if you like. Ham, gruyère, and a fried egg is the classic.
- **Sweet fillings**: Butter and sugar with a squeeze of lemon (the Paris street-stand classic), Nutella and banana, jam, or berries and whipped cream. For a folded "crêpe Suzette" style, brush with orange butter and a little Grand Marnier.
- **Brown butter**: Brown the 56 g butter before adding it to the blender for a nuttier crêpe.
- **Richer or lighter**: For thinner, lacier crêpes, swap more of the milk for water, up to half. For more tender, richer crêpes, use 2% milk plus an extra egg yolk.
